Why heat pumps are winning in cold climates

Marta Lindqvist

For decades the advice was simple: if your winters drop well below freezing, a heat pump is not for you. That advice is now out of date, and the numbers coming out of Norway, Finland and the northern United States show how quickly it has aged.

Cold-climate models from several manufacturers keep delivering useful heat at minus 25 °C. The trick is a variable-speed compressor paired with refrigerants that stay efficient when the air outside has little warmth left to give.

Installers in Maine report that the question has shifted from whether a heat pump will cope to how large a unit a house really needs. Oversizing, once a safety margin, now mostly means paying for capacity that sits idle.

The economics follow the engineering. A typical home switching from oil heating saves between $300 and $900 a year, according to the study.
